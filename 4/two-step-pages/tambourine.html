Tambourine

Tambourines are classified as struck membranophones and indirectly struck idiophones. When being played as a membranophone, they make sound by being hit by a hand where sound then vibrates off the membrane and the jingles. When being played as an idiophone, they make sound by being shaken where sound then vibrates off the jingles. Tambourines are typically held and played by hand.

Classical Applications

Tambourines are used to add bright rhythmic accents to a piece. They are similar to the triangle and bells in this way, however they also have their membrane which, combined with the jingles, makes a more unique sound for the purposes of orchestra.

Famous Piece

The famous opera Carmen uses the tambourine in Habanera to accent in between the main singer's solo. The tambourine gives the supporting chorus more of a pop in the few moments they have.

Fun Fact!

Tambourines can come headless (without a membrane). These are simply shaken to be played through the jingles.
