Violin

Violins are classified as composite chordophones. They make sound by using a bowed string against strung strings which vibrate through the hollow body of the instrument. The violin is played by putting the base of the instrument on your shoulder (holding the other end with your hand) and moving the bow across.

Classical Applications

The violin provides an expressive range that leads the orchestra. Over time, there has been less reliance on the violin and melodies have been spread out. However, there used to be a time when the violin was the melody and heart of every orchestra. Today, it still plays a foundation part in establishing chords and giving the orchestra its distinct sound.

Famous Piece

Vivaldi's The Four Seasons is a set of violin concertos. Among them are many classic tunes that have become world renowned. One of the most well known is Winter I. The violins here builds up suspense in quick pluckings ending with a brilliant violin solo.

Fun Fact!

Due to the motion required to play the instrument, a violinist burns around 170 calories per hour while playing.
