Snare Drum

Snare drums are classified as struck membranophones. They make sound by being hit by a stick where sound then vibrates off the snare wires that are attached to the bottom membrane. The addition of the snare wires (simply metal wires strung under the instrument) give snares a very unique sound that is distinct from other percussion instruments.

Classical Applications

Snare drums typically take the role of accenting and dramatization for the purpose of orchestra. They can add suspense with its unique soundscape and lead into other instruments well. A well placed snare can have the effect of making a piece seem more militaristic and structured. Snares can have solos and main melodies, although this is more common in traditional bands and marching bands.

Famous Piece

Whiplash is a movie about drumming and taking things to the extreme. It follows a very emotional story that ends with a fantastic drum solo. This is a part of that solo that focuses on the snare drum and uses it to set up tension and anticipation.

Fun Fact!

Snare drums evolved from an instrument known as a Tabor. This instrument was key in militaristic operations as it was used to communicate and send signals across battlefields.
